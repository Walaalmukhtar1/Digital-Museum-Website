Meet the Minds Behind the Masterpieces

Discover the stories, inspirations, and creative journeys of the artists who bring our digital museum to life.

VICTORIA POND

Victoria Pond is a professional artist based near the Helford Passage in West Cornwall. Having spent most of her life in rural North Wales and Cornwall, she has always appreciated the beauty of her natural surroundings and loves to immerse herself in nature sketching and painting from it, with the aim of communicating something of the feeling of that immersive experience. The results are expressive, both bold and sensitive.

Drawing from observation is the foundation of Victoria’s creative practice. When working in a more visceral way, whether abstracting the landscape or extracting it from the imagination, instinct is more of the essence than intellectual perception.

Victoria uses various surfaces and mediums, she believes that different materials and surfaces lend themselves to different techniques, marks and moods and to tie herself to one, would be to limit herself and her work.

PHIL WHITING

Phil Whiting was born in Hull and as a young man attended Falmouth Colleges of Art, where he graduated in Fine Art. His paintings, like the present untitled work, of the post-industrial landscapes of West Cornwall are arresting and powerfully composed.

The artist has travelled and worked extensively including to Ground Zero, Auschwitz-Birkenau, Bosnia, Syria and Lebanon

In 2017 Phil Whiting was the winner of the Red Line Art Works Annual Award. The citation for this award stated: 'Phil's art incorporates big events and issues, their awful consequences and our responsibilities for them. His work raises tough questions about what we humans have done, what we have failed to do, what we have looked away from, who we really are, what we should be. This is rare and powerful art which penetrates and can leave its mark on us.'

LOUISE LEVINE

I’m a semi-abstract acrylic seascape artist, inspired by the rugged, beautiful Cornish coastline. I adore being by the sea in Cornwall, feeling the immense vastness of the ocean and the peace brought by the rhythm of the waves. It brings me a feeling of space and calm as if I’ve come home and can breathe deeply again. It’s this wild peace, raw beauty and vast space that I encapsulate in my work to allow you to feel that connection to the sea in your own home, whenever you need it.

CHRISTINE BRUNNOCK

Christine Brunnock is a St Ives artist fascinated by the interaction of the elemental and opposing forces of the Atlantic ocean and the Cornish landscape.

This is wonderfully captured in this simple but powerful artwork. In 'Resilience' the artist has used a mix of acrylic paint, netting and metal leaf; the latter to capture the fluorescence of the sea water and wet rock. If you've ever looked down from a Cornish cliff at the boiling ocean below, you'll recognise the honest expression of this work which uses the mix of media so cleverly.

RICHARD LANNOWE HALL

Richard Lannowe Hall moved to Cornwall in 1996 pursuing his joint passions for sailing and painting seascapes. A student of light and its transformative effect upon water, Hall ably sustains the tradition of impressionist painting of Cornwall's famous coast and unique light. The dramatic manner in which he captures the sea and north coast light, whilst very different, echoes the same fascination and fearlessness that we see in the work of Julius Olsson a century earlier.

Hall studied at Guildford College of Art, North East London College of Art and the Royal Academy of Art, before settling in Cornwall where he has exhibited regularly.

JOHN McKINSTRY

I am a Cornwall based Artist and I have been painting the landscape for as long as I remember. It is something I just can’t imagine not doing, and like most Landscape artists I try to capture those parts of it that most inspire. For me that is solitary cottages, small hamlets and harbours clinging to the coast, or moorland farms stark against imposing skies, these are the places that stir in me the emotions I need to feel while I paint and which I hope I manage to occasionally reflect on my canvas.

I have worked in various forms of Horticulture for over 30 years and in various parts of the country and enjoyed it immensely but in 2013 I had the opportunity to fulfil a life long dream and become a full time Landscape artist, signing with the UK’s leading Art publisher Demontfort Fine Art, supplying galleries right across the UK and also Internationally with my unique style of calming art, during which time I was rather busy selling over 750 Original paintings as well as building a world wide collectors base.

In September of 2022 I became an independent artist, giving myself a more flexible approach to my painting and a more hands on relationship with the collectors of my art.

GRAHAM HALL MNM

Graham Hall MNM, originally from Harrogate, North Yorkshire, trained at Gravesend Sea School for a Merchant Navy career, which spanned over 37 years. He sailed with notable passenger lines such as Cunard and Union Castle, and later on salvage tugs, painting during long voyages to pass the time.

In 2004, Graham retired from the sea to focus on painting and settled in Falmouth with his partner, Penny Phillips. His notable works include paintings commissioned for the RFA Mounts Bay, presented to Lady Sarah Band, and the RFA Argus, for which he received commendation from the Royal Society of Marine Artists. He has also been commissioned by HRH Duke of Edinburgh, the Brazilian Navy, and other maritime entities, alongside creating landscapes, still life, wildlife, and seascapes.

Graham co-runs The Mission to Seafarers in Falmouth Docks, supporting visiting seafarers with communication and creating an award-winning garden. For his voluntary work, he was awarded the Merchant Navy Medal in 2014.

ELEANOR MARY HUGHES, 1882-1959

Eleanor Hughes (née Waymouth), born in 1882 in Christchurch, New Zealand to West Country parents, was a prominent figure in the Lamorna artist colony. A close friend of Laura and Harold Knight, Lamorna Birch, and other notable artists, she studied art in Britain under C. N. Worsley and at the Forbes School of Painting. In 1907, she returned to Newlyn to study under Stanhope and Elizabeth Forbes, where she met and married fellow artist Robert Morson Hughes in 1910.

The couple designed and built their home, Chyangweal, near St Buryan, and Eleanor also maintained a studio in the Lamorna Valley. Known for her delicate style, often compared to Rennie Mackintosh, her works were characterized by visible pencil lines under and over watercolour washes, adding texture and detail, particularly to her depictions of trees.

Eleanor began exhibiting at the Royal Academy in 1911 and later at the Royal Institute of Painters in Watercolours, signing her works under her married name from 1912. In 1933, she became a full member of the R.I. and actively organized exhibitions in Newlyn and St. Ives. Eleanor remained a close friend of Dame Laura Knight throughout her life and passed away at her Lamorna home in 1959.

CHRIS PEACOCK

Born in 1950 in Bristol, I have spent most of my adult life in Cornwall, a place I first discovered in 1977 as a Royal Navy draftee. Captivated by the vibrant hedgerows and shimmering turquoise seas, I felt an immediate connection to this special place, which I now call home.

My journey into painting began later in life, inspired by the guidance and encouragement of artist and teacher Jenny Aitken. Her mentorship, coupled with participation in workshops with diverse artists, helped me develop my artistic voice and confidence. My work predominantly focuses on Cornish coastal landscapes, capturing the essence of this region's inseparable connection to the sea. Using acrylics or oils on surfaces like canvas, MDF, or heavy-quality paper, I aim to distill the beauty of Cornwall in my paintings.

I consider myself a passionate artist rather than a commercial one, painting for the love of it while welcoming others' appreciation of my work. My style is naive in detail yet emphasizes the essential elements that bring cohesion to the final image. Looking ahead, I’m excited to experiment with abstracting landscapes, starting with a stretch of coastline near me. Beyond that, I am open to wherever the artistic journey leads, embracing the vast possibilities of the art world.
